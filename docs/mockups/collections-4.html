Collections

Lists I keep and numbers I keep counting. None of them is finished.

One shelf, always the page's width. At rest the ten spines share the width equally (121px each at 1440). The one you point at grows and the rest give up width, down to a 72px floor, so the shelf never changes size and never has an empty end. It scales to about fifteen collections before spines hit the floor. The cost: the closed spines are the narrowest of any variant, and the name drops to 32px.

Two shelves of five. Spines keep a fixed, comfortable width (112px) and wrap, so each opens at full size and nothing squeezes. Each shelf opens independently. Nothing moves on the other shelf. A long list is just more shelves, so it never runs out, but the page gets tall and the order is the only grouping.

Shelves with section labels. The same fixed spines, grouped by what kind of collection it is (Places and nature, Taste, Things), each shelf under a section label and its rule. Ten collections become three short lists, which is easier to scan and gives the page structure. It asks you to name the groups, and for a collection to belong to exactly one.

Books of different heights. Same fill behaviour as 1, but each spine's height follows the square root of how many entries it holds, standing on the shelf line, so the shelf has a skyline: the life list is tall, city guides and cameras short. The open spine rises to full height. It is the most personality and the least dashboard-like way to hint at size. The cost: height now means something, so it must stay subtle, and a collection with few entries looks minor.
